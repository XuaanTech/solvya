## How old are you, exactly?

If someone asks your age, you probably answer "25 years old." But 25 years — how many **months**? How many **days**? If you were born on September 14, 2001, then on September 14, 2026 you are exactly 25 years, zero months and zero days. On any other day of the year, your real age is a combination, like "24 years, 7 months and 3 days."

Knowing your exact age matters more than it seems: for **legal documents**, **employment contracts**, **visa applications** or **medical records**, whole years are not enough; precise dates and figures are required.

## Why calculate the exact age?

There are situations where the exact age is a requirement, not a curiosity:

- **Legal procedures**: contracts, notary documents, emancipation and tax obligations.
- **Medical records**: age in days is crucial in pediatrics and pediatric dosing.
- **Human resources**: retirement, seniority and benefits calculations.
- **Sports and competitions**: age categories with exact cutoff months.
- **Personal milestones**: anniversaries, big birthdays and life goals.

In all of these, **subtracting two dates correctly** is what separates a reliable figure from a rough guess.

## How the calculation works

The most common mistake is to subtract the years: "2026 − 2001 = 25 years." But that ignores months and days. The correct calculation works like this:

1. **Compare day and month**: if today's day and month have passed your birth day and month, you have had a birthday this year; otherwise, you are still one year younger.
2. **Subtract the months**: adjust the month count depending on whether you have reached your birth day in the current month.
3. **Count the real days**: elapsed days are counted against the **real calendar**, month by month.

And here comes the leap-year detail: every four years, February has 29 days instead of 28. A calculation that simply multiplied by 365 would "lose" a day every four years. A proper calculator adds each year's real days, including the extra leap day.

> **Tip**: if you were born on February 29 (a leap day), your legal birthday in non-leap years is usually celebrated on February 28 or March 1, depending on the country.

| ---------- | ---------------- | --------- | ---------- |
| 01/01/2000 | 14/09/2026 | 26 years, 8 months and 13 days | 9,753 days |
| 01/01/1990 | 14/09/2026 | 36 years, 8 months and 13 days | 13,406 days |
| 14/09/2001 | 14/09/2026 | 25 years exactly | 9,131 days |
| 01/03/2000 | 01/03/2026 | 26 years exactly | 9,498 days |
| 14/09/2020 | 14/09/2026 | 6 years exactly | 2,192 days |

Notice that the 6-year example (2020-2026) includes two leap years — 2020 and 2024 — which is why it comes to 2,192 days rather than 2,190. **These small details are what make a calculation exact.**

> **Fun fact**: someone born on January 1, 1970 (the famous Unix epoch date) would be 56 years old in 2026, with more than 20,700 days lived.

## Total days, weeks and months

Think about your life in smaller units and the numbers get impressive:

- **25 years** means about **9,131 days** lived.
- Those days equal roughly **1,304 total weeks**.
- And about **300 total months** of life.
- By age 40 you will have passed **14,600 days**.

> **Fun fact**: you pass the **one million hours** mark at age 114 — a milestone very few people reach.

## Conclusion

Knowing your exact age in years, months and days is not just curiosity: it matters for paperwork, medicine, sports and personal milestones. Calculating it by hand with leap years and months of different lengths is error-prone, so the smart move is to let a calculator count every real day.
