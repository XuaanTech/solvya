Converting **units of measurement** is an everyday skill: kilometres to miles when travelling, grams to ounces in a recipe, or Celsius to Fahrenheit when reading the weather in another country. The rules are few and by learning the key factors you can solve almost any conversion in seconds.

## The metric system: everything is powers of 10

The advantage of the international system is that prefixes work the same for metres, grams and litres:

| Prefix | Symbol | Factor |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Kilo | k | × 1,000 |
| Hecto | h | × 100 |
| Deca | da | × 10 |
| (base) | — | × 1 |
| Deci | d | ÷ 10 |
| Centi | c | ÷ 100 |
| Milli | m | ÷ 1,000 |

**Trick:** to go from one prefix to another, count the jumps. From **km to m** it is 3 jumps (k→base): multiply by 1,000. From **g to mg** also 3 jumps: multiply by 1,000.

## Length: metres, kilometres and more

Essential equivalences:

- 1 km = 1,000 m
- 1 m = 100 cm = 1,000 mm
- 1 m ≈ 1.0936 yards
- 1 km ≈ 0.6214 miles
- 1 inch = 2.54 cm

**Example:** How many metres are 12 km? → 12 × 1,000 = **12,000 m**.

## Mass: grams and kilograms

- 1 kg = 1,000 g
- 1 g = 1,000 mg
- 1 tonne = 1,000 kg
- 1 kg ≈ 2.2046 pounds
- 1 ounce = 28.3495 g
- 1 pound = 453.592 g

**Example:** 3.5 kg in grams → 3.5 × 1,000 = **3,500 g**.

## Volume: litres and millilitres

- 1 L = 1,000 mL
- 1 L = 1 dm³ = 0.001 m³
- 1 mL = 1 cm³
- 1 US gallon = 3.785 L
- 1 L ≈ 0.2642 US gallons

**Useful tip:** 1 litre of water weighs exactly 1 kg. That is the equivalence that links volume and mass in the metric system.

## Temperature: the exception

Temperatures are **not** converted with simple factors because their scales start at different points. Memorise these three formulas:

| Conversion | Formula |
|------------|---------|
| °F → °C | °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9 |
| °C → °F | °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32 |
| °C → K | K = °C + 273.15 |
| K → °C | °C = K − 273.15 |

**Example:** 68 °F in °C → (68 − 32) × 5/9 = 36 × 5/9 = **20 °C**.

## Common conversion mistakes

1. **Confusing mass and weight**: in cooking, grams and millilitres are not the same except for water.
2. **Forgetting that temperature is not linear**: 20 °C is not "twice" 10 °C on an absolute scale.
3. **Getting the number of zeros right**: from km to cm is **5 jumps** (×100,000), not 3.
4. **Rounding too early**: round at the end of the calculation, not at every step.
