**Kelvin (K)** is the **absolute temperature scale** of the International System. Unlike Celsius and Fahrenheit, it **starts at absolute zero** (0 K), where all thermal motion ceases. Here you have the **exact formulas**, **wide table**, **mental trick** and **key uses**: science, **color temperature** (lighting/photography) and **cryogenics**.

## Why Kelvin is special

| Scale | Zero | Degree size | Symbol |
|-------|------|-------------|--------|
| **Kelvin** | Absolute zero (0 K) | 1 K = 1 °C | **K** (no °) |
| **Celsius** | Water freezes (0 °C) | 1 °C = 1 K | °C |
| **Fahrenheit** | Ice/salt mix (0 °F) | 1 °F = 5/9 K | °F |

> **0 K = −273.15 °C = −459.67 °F** (absolute zero).  
> **273.15 K = 0 °C = 32 °F** (ice).  
> **373.15 K = 100 °C = 212 °F** (water boils).

## Exact formulas

### Kelvin ↔ Celsius (fixed offset)
> **°C = K − 273.15**  
> **K = °C + 273.15**

### Kelvin ↔ Fahrenheit (via Celsius)
> **°F = (K − 273.15) × 1.8 + 32**  
> **K = (°F − 32) / 1.8 + 273.15**

## Color temperature (lighting, photography, screens)

**Color temperature** is expressed **in Kelvin** (ideal black body). It's not thermal temperature, it's the color of light.

| K | Appearance | Typical use |
|---|------------|-------------|
| 1700–1900 | Deep red/amber | Candle, flame |
| 2000–2500 | Warm orange | Vintage bulb, sunset |
| 2700–3000 | Warm white | Home, restaurants, "warm white" |
| 3500–4100 | Neutral white | Offices, retail, "neutral white" |
| 5000–5500 | Daylight (D50/D55) | Graphic arts, photography, "daylight" |
| 6000–6500 | Cool white / Daylight | sRGB monitors, overcast, "cool white" |
| 7000–10000 | Bluish | Shade, blue sky, "north light" |

> **Rule**: **Low K = warm (red/amber)**; **High K = cool (blue)**.  
> In LED: "2700 K" = warm; "6500 K" = cool.

## Mental trick

> **°C ≈ K − 273** (error 0.15 °C, negligible except precision metrology)

| K | Trick °C | Exact °C |
|---|----------|----------|
| 300 | 27 | 26.85 |
| 293 | 20 | 19.85 |
| 310 | 37 | 36.85 |
| 5500 | 5227 | 5226.85 |

For **color temperature** (2000–10000 K) the approximation **is enough**.
