Converting **euros to dollars (EUR/USD)** seems trivial, but the **real cost** depends on **where** and **how** you do it. The rate you see on Google (**mid-market**) isn't what you get. Here we explain **how the pair works**, **where fees hide**, **when to exchange** and the **best alternatives** today.

## What is the EUR/USD rate

**EUR/USD = how many dollars (USD) you get for 1 euro (EUR).**  
Example: EUR/USD = 1.0850 → 1 € = 1.0850 $.

- **Base**: EUR (always 1).
- **Quote**: USD (variable).
- **Rising** = euro strengthens (you get more $ per €).
- **Falling** = dollar strengthens.

The **Forex** market runs 24 h / 5 days (Sunday 22:00 UTC – Friday 22:00 UTC). Weekends **closed** → spreads widen.

## Mid-market rate vs. client rate

| Rate | What it is | Who sees it | Typical spread |
|------|------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Mid-market (interbank)** | Mid-price between bank buy/sell | Google, XE, Reuters, Bloomberg | 0 % (reference) |
| **Retail client (bank)** | Mid-market + spread + fixed fee | Your online banking, branch | **1.5–3 %** + €3–15/op |
| **Fintech / multi-currency** | Mid-market + reduced spread | Revolut, Wise, N26, Trade Republic, Evo, MyInvestor | **0.3–0.7 %** (sometimes 0 % in market hours) |
| **Airport / hotel** | Mid-market + high spread | Physical exchange bureaus | **5–10 %** |

> **Golden rule**: **never exchange cash at the airport**. You lose €50–100 per €1000.

## Where costs hide

1. **Spread (margin over mid-market)**: the difference between buy and sell. Banks: 1.5–3 %. Fintechs: 0.3–0.7 %.
2. **Fixed fee per transaction**: €3–15 at traditional banks.
3. **DCC (Dynamic Currency Conversion)**: the ATM/POS offers "charge in EUR". **Say no**. The merchant/bank sets the rate, usually 3–7 % worse.
4. **Destination ATM fee**: $2–5 per withdrawal + possible fee from your bank.
5. **Weekends / holidays**: Revolut and others apply a **0.5–1 % surcharge** (market closed, they hedge risk).

## When to exchange: basic timing

- **Market hours (Mon–Fri)**: tightest spreads. Avoid early morning (low liquidity).
- **Weekends**: market closed → widened spreads / fintech surcharges. If you can, wait for Monday.
- **Macro news (ECB, Fed, CPI, payrolls)**: volatility ↑ → spreads ↑. Avoid trading the hour of the release unless speculating.
- **Average in**: if you'll spend $2000 over 2 weeks, exchange $500 every 3–4 days (dollar-cost averaging) → reduces bad-day risk.

## Checklist before travel / buying in USD

1. ✅ Have a **no-FX-fee card** (Revolut, Wise, N26, Trade Republic, Evo, MyInvestor).
2. ✅ **Disable DCC**: at ATM/POS, **always choose local currency (USD)**.
3. ✅ Carry **some USD cash** (100–200 $) for tips/small cash-only payments.
4. ✅ Notify your bank of dates/country (avoids fraud blocks).
5. ✅ Check **daily ATM withdrawal limits** (usually €500–1000/day).
6. ✅ Use **Apple Pay / Google Pay** with the no-fee card: safer, same rate.

## Conclusion

- **Real rate** = mid-market (Google/XE).
- **Your cost** = mid-market + spread + fees.
- **Win**: no-FX-fee card + pay in local currency (USD) + avoid airport/DCC.
- **Typical saving**: €30–80 per €1000 exchanged vs traditional bank.
