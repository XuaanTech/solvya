**Roman numerals** are still found in centuries, royal names, book chapters, the Olympic Games and some clocks, but their rules are not always remembered. Here is the **complete guide**: symbols, reading rules, reference table, common mistakes and an **automatic converter** at the end.

## The seven basic symbols

| Symbol | Value | Origin |
|--------|-------|--------|
| I | 1 | finger (unus) |
| V | 5 | open hand (quinque) |
| X | 10 | two crossed hands (decem) |
| L | 50 | (quinquaginta) |
| C | 100 | centum |
| D | 500 | (quingenti) |
| M | 1000 | mille |

With these seven symbols you can write **any number from 1 to 3999** in classical notation.

## The two reading rules

> **Rule 1 — Addition:** when a symbol is followed by one of **equal or lesser** value, add them. Ex: VI = 6, LX = 60, MC = 1100.
> **Rule 2 — Subtraction:** when a symbol is followed by one of **greater** value, subtract. Ex: IV = 4, XC = 90, CM = 900.

**Only six subtractions are valid:**

| Subtraction | Value | Subtraction | Value |
|-------------|-------|-------------|-------|
| IV | 4 | XL | 40 |
| IX | 9 | XC | 90 |
| CD | 400 | CM | 900 |

For example, IL for 49 and IC for 99 are wrong; the correct forms are **XLIX** and **XCIX**.

## Most common mistakes

- ❌ **IIII** for 4 → the correct form is **IV** (IIII only appears on clocks by tradition).
- ❌ **VV** for 10 → the correct form is **X**; V, L and D are never repeated.
- ❌ **IIIII** for 5 → the correct form is **V**; any symbol is repeated at most **three times**.
- ❌ **IC** for 99 → the correct form is **XCIX**; only the six pairs above are used for subtraction.
