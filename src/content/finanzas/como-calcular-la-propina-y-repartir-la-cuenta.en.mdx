You have just had a great meal and the bill arrives. How much **tip** should you leave? How do you split the check fairly when several people are involved and not everyone ordered the same thing? This guide clarifies international tipping customs, the formulas for calculating a tip and the best methods for splitting the bill without friction.

## What Percentage of Tip Should You Leave?

There is no single rule that applies to every country. Tipping customs vary enormously by local culture:

- **Spain and Portugal:** Tipping is **optional**. In mid-range bars and restaurants, many customers round up or leave **5–10%** if the service was excellent. In fine-dining restaurants, a gratuity is more expected.
- **United States and Canada:** Tipping is nearly **mandatory** because much of the server's income depends on it. The norm is **15–20%** of the pre-tax amount. Leaving less than 15% is seen as a sign of dissatisfaction.
- **United Kingdom and Ireland:** Customs sit in between. If service is not included, the usual amount is **10–15%**. Many restaurants add an optional service charge of 10–12%.
- **Japan and South Korea:** Leaving a tip can even be considered **offensive**, since good service is simply part of the job.
- **Latin America and Central Europe:** Ranges from about **5% to 15%** depending on the country and type of establishment. In tourist areas, the expectation is usually higher.

## How to Calculate the Tip

The formula is very direct:

> **Tip = Bill × (Percentage / 100)**
> **Total to pay = Bill + Tip**

For example, if the bill is **120** and you want to leave **10%**:
Tip = 120 × 0.10 = **12**. Total = **132**.

If you are traveling in the USA and the check is **75** with an **18%** tip:
Tip = 75 × 0.18 = **13.50**. Total = **88.50**.

> **Traveler's tip:** Before tipping abroad, check whether the bill already includes a **service charge** (*service charge* or *servizio incluso*). If it does, no additional tip is needed unless the service was exceptional.

## Splitting the Bill Among Several People

Dividing the check equitably avoids misunderstandings. There are two approaches:

### 1. Equal split (recommended when orders are similar)

> **Amount per person = (Bill + Tip) / Number of people**

If there are **6 people** with a bill of 240 and a tip of 24 (10%), each person pays 264 / 6 = **44**.

### 2. Proportional split (when orders differ greatly)

Write down what each person ordered and allocate the tip **proportionally to each person's consumption**:
Each person pays their own consumption + (their consumption / total) × tip.

This is fair when one person ordered a light dish and another ordered an expensive bottle of wine.

## Common Errors

- **Calculating the percentage on the total including tax when you should use the subtotal.** In the USA, tips are calculated on the **pre-tax** amount; using the full total inflates it unnecessarily.
- **Forgetting to include the tip when splitting.** Many people divide only the bill and leave the tip aside, which complicates the math and creates imbalance.
- **Assuming the tip is already included without checking.** In some countries the service charge is optional even when it appears on the receipt — you can ask for it to be removed if service was poor.
- **Rounding incorrectly when splitting.** If you divide 100 between 3 people, each owes **33.33** — not 33, because then 1 is missing. Always round up or use a calculator.

## Conclusion

Calculating a tip and splitting the bill does not have to be a source of stress or arguments. Know the appropriate percentage for the country you are in, apply the right formula and choose the splitting method that best fits your group.
