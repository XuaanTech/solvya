**Percentages** are everywhere: sale discounts, restaurant tips, taxes on your paycheck and statistics in the news. Knowing how to calculate them quickly saves you time and prevents costly mistakes. This guide covers the three most common percentage operations with clear formulas, a quick-reference table and errors to avoid.

## The Three Most Common Operations

Although calculating percentages seems straightforward, in practice most people encounter three distinct scenarios.

### 1. Calculate X% of a Number

This is the most common operation. You want to know, for example, **20% of 150**. The formula is:

> **Result = Number × (Percentage / 100)**

So: 150 × (20/100) = 150 × 0.20 = **30**.

This operation is useful for calculating store discounts, restaurant tips or sales commissions.

### 2. What Percentage Is One Number of Another

Suppose you sold **45 out of 60 products**. What percentage of the total is that? The formula is:

> **Percentage = (Part / Total) × 100**

Applying it: (45/60) × 100 = **75%**.

This calculation helps measure performance, analyze budgets or compare metrics across periods.

### 3. Percentage Change Between Two Values

If your revenue went from **80,000 to 96,000**, you need the **growth percentage**. The formula is:

> **Change % = ((New value - Original value) / Original value) × 100**

Calculating: ((96,000 - 80,000) / 80,000) × 100 = **20% growth**.

A negative result means a **percentage decrease**. This operation is essential for analyzing investments, prices or economic indicators.

Here are common calculations you can use as a template:

| Operation | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 20% of 150 | 150 × 0.20 | **30** |
| 10% of 500 | 500 × 0.10 | **50** |
| 25% of 320 | 320 × 0.25 | **80** |

## Formula Summary

The three core formulas in compact form:

1. **X% of a number:** `A × (X / 100)`
2. **A is what % of B:** `(A / B) × 100`
3. **Percentage change:** `((New - Original) / Original) × 100`

> **Tip:** For quick mental math, common percentages like 10%, 25% and 50% are easy: 10% means shifting the decimal one place left, 25% means dividing by 4 and 50% means dividing by 2.

## Common Errors

Even experienced people make percentage mistakes. The most frequent ones are:

- **Confusing "of" with "more than."** Calculating 20% **of** 150 (30) is not the same as adding 20% **to** 150 (180). They are completely different operations.
- **Dividing the wrong way in comparisons.** To find what percentage 75 is of 200, divide 75 by 200, not the other way around. The partial number always goes in the **numerator**.
- **Calculating the discount and forgetting the final price.** If a product costs 200 and has a 15% discount, the savings are 30, but the final price is **170**, not 30.
- **Adding and subtracting percentages directly.** If a price rises 10% and then drops 10%, you do not return to the original price. You end up at **99%** of the starting price because the second percentage applies to a larger base.

> **Remember:** Percentages are always calculated on a reference base. Identifying that base before doing the math is the key to getting it right.
