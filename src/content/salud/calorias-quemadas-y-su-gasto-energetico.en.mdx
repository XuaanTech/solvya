How many calories do you burn **walking, running or cycling**? There is no single number — it depends on your **weight**, the **intensity** and the **time**. The **calories burned calculator** uses the **MET** (metabolic equivalent) method to give you a quick, practical estimate. It helps if you want to plan a calorie deficit, compare activities, or just satisfy your curiosity about the energy cost of your daily routine.

## What is MET?

**MET** (*Metabolic Equivalent of Task*) is a standard unit that measures the intensity of a physical activity by comparing it to your resting expenditure.

- **1 MET** = your body's expenditure **at complete rest** (lying down, doing nothing).
- If an activity has **5 MET**, it burns **5 times** what you burn at rest.

The formula to estimate per-minute expenditure is:

> **kcal/min = MET × 3.5 × weight(kg) / 200**

**Example:** a **70 kg** person running (MET 9.8) for **30 minutes**:

- kcal/min = 9.8 × 3.5 × 70 / 200 ≈ **12.0 kcal/min**
- Total = 12.0 × 30 ≈ **360 kcal**

> The value 3.5 ml O₂/kg/min is the standard resting oxygen consumption; the factor 200 converts the result to kcal.

## Activity table and MET values

These are approximate METs for common activities, with the estimated per-minute burn for a **70 kg** person:

| Activity | MET | Calories/min (70 kg) |
|----------|-----|----------------------|
| Rest (lying down) | 1.3 | 1.6 kcal/min |
| Slow walking (stroll) | 3.3 | 4.0 kcal/min |
| Brisk walking (5–6 km/h) | 4.3 | 5.3 kcal/min |
| Running (8–9 km/h) | 9.8 | 12.0 kcal/min |
| Cycling (moderate, 15–20 km/h) | 8.0 | 9.8 kcal/min |
| Swimming (moderate pace) | 8.0 | 9.8 kcal/min |
| Strength training | 6.0 | 7.4 kcal/min |
| Football (match) | 7.0 | 8.6 kcal/min |
| Stair climbing | 6.0 | 7.4 kcal/min |
| Aerobics (general class) | 6.5 | 8.0 kcal/min |

MET values are population averages; your actual burn varies with technique, terrain, gradient or whether you are on flat ground.

## Factors that affect real expenditure

MET is a population average; your real number shifts with these factors:

- **Actual intensity**: a light jog is not the same as an all-out run even though both are "running".
- **Muscle mass**: more muscle = higher basal burn and higher cost per movement.
- **Temperature and humidity**: extreme heat or cold raises expenditure (thermoregulation).
- **Fitness level**: at the same speed, a trained person burns less because they are more efficient.
- **Age and sex**: they influence basal metabolism and body composition.

> That is why the calculator gives an **indicative estimate**, not a precise figure. Use it as a guide to compare options and plan, not as exact accounting.

## Conclusion

The MET method is a practical tool to **estimate your energy expenditure** without a lab. Combine your weight, the activity and the time, and in seconds you have a figure you can work with: plan your nutrition, compare what burns more in less time, or simply understand your energy balance better.
